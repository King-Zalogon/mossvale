# Character art sources

Generated with the image generation tool on 2026-10-03, then exported to fixed 160 × 256 RGBA cells. The source PNGs are kept here with the exact brief summaries and reference asset IDs; the export script uses nearest-neighbor scaling, normalizes each sprite to a fixed height, and aligns its feet to y=250.

Each generation sheet used the same brief: create a transparent 5-column × 2-row sprite sheet of the existing red-cap adventurer, at the original isometric view, palette, pixel scale, clothing and lighting; use one direction per row, idle followed by four compact alternating walk poses; keep character size, torso, head, backpack and foot line fixed; leave gaps between sprites; no body bob, shadows, scenery, labels or background. The row directions for each source image are listed above. Diagonal references used the nearest cardinal reference with the prior generated batch as a style reference.

The v1 neutral idle source was generated on 2026-10-07. For #324, NE/NW/SW idle cells were corrected from the retained direction sources and source transform while preserving the other five cells. East received a new five-pose walk strip. NE keeps its diagonal head/torso through a four-pose gait whose foot positions are distinct at gameplay size; NW is its horizontal mirror. All 40 exported cells were inspected at the 52 × 84 px gameplay draw size and against the renderer preview. The NE/NW walk directions keep one facing through every frame, East has four distinct gait cells, and all exported sprites share the y=250 foot anchor. Export checks verify dimensions, transparent padding, baseline, distinct East gait frames and minimum diagonal foot travel; these checks supplement visual review instead of trying to infer facing from pixels.

## Export and metadata

Run `python3 art/characters/export.py` with Pillow installed. `metadata.json` records frame order, dimensions, bottom-center anchors, and walk cadence. Open `dist/character-preview.html` to inspect every player pose and NPC facing at gameplay scale, including the production renderer fixture. The PNGs are flattened generated source sheets rather than layered Aseprite documents; use the retained source grids and export script for future edits.

## Creature animation treatment

The original twelve creature portraits remain source cells in `art/assets/source/creatures-atlas.png`; Sedgegnaw, Petalunge and Cindercurl add three generated portrait sources normalized into `art/assets/source/creature-additions-atlas.png`. All seventeen outputs are recorded in `art/assets/metadata.json`. A shared animation treatment applies to every manifest creature, so newly added species inherit it without per-species code: idle breathing, distance-paced travel bob, event-driven hit recoil and capture draw-in. The treatment leaves the art pixels and ground anchor unchanged. State methods, cadence and reduced-motion behavior are recorded in [`creature-animation-metadata.json`](creature-animation-metadata.json).

## Creature combat frame milestone

- Fernling and Duskwing were generated on 2026-10-03 as the first combat-sheet milestone. Brooklet and Hushram were added on 2026-10-03 as the next art batch. All four raw generated images are retained under `source/creature-*-combat-generated.png` and use their current transparent portrait as the identity reference.
- Emberkin and Voltkit combat sheets add two more species. Their immutable generated PNG bytes are stored in bounded parts referenced by `source-archive.json`; `source_archive.py` verifies and streams those exact bytes to the exporter, and `restore-generated-sources.py` can reconstruct the original PNG files for editing.
- Mushmallow and Frostowl add the next two combat species. Their transparent source atlases use the same four-column, five-state grid and preserve their canonical mushroom-hedgehog and ice-owl identities; exact PNG bytes are retained as SHA-256-checked 600 KB archive chunks. The 256-color runtime exports use no dithering and keep the shared 288 × 288 frame and foot-anchor contract.
- Each sheet has four columns and five state rows: idle, attack, hit, faint and capture. The exporter crops equal grid cells, pads transparent pixels without resampling, and writes fixed frame sizes with bottom-center anchors: Fernling, Brooklet and Hushram 288 × 288; Duskwing 281 × 281.
- Emberkin and Voltkit runtime sheets use a 256-color indexed PNG palette with no dithering; their complete original RGBA source sheets remain unchanged in the archive. Run `python3 art/characters/export-creature-combat.py` to recreate runtime PNGs. `creature-combat-metadata.json` records source IDs, original hashes, frame order, dimensions, anchor and cadence.
- Pebblit, Bramblebuck, Siltkip and Sunskitter complete the combat roster. Their 4×5 transparent sources use species-specific frame profiles where needed to preserve the generated pixels and shared ground anchor. Fernling's revised faint row progressively lowers the creature to a lying pose; the prior detached leaf fragments are removed.
- All seventeen current species have five combat states. `make-combat-contact-sheet.py` inspects each source at 115 px beside its canonical portrait. The optional combat manifest IDs retain the static portrait as fallback; state timing and reduced-motion behavior remain renderer controlled.

## Creature follower direction milestone (#90)

- Emberkin and Fernling overworld follower sheets were generated on 2026-10-03 from their current transparent creature portraits. The retained `*-follower-generated.png` sheets are the direction-preserving, margin-corrected generation outputs; `*-follower-initial.png` retains the first generated draft for review history.
- Duskwing and Brooklet follow-up sheets were generated on 2026-10-03 from their current transparent portraits, with the combat atlases used only as secondary identity references. The generated source sheets are retained as `creature-duskwing-follower-generated.png` and `creature-brooklet-follower-generated.png`; Brooklet's undersized second-row pose was revised before export. These are overworld sheets and remain separate from the combat art.
- Hushram and Voltkit follower sheets extend the batch. Their exact generated PNG bytes are retained in `source-archive.json` chunks and verified by SHA-256 before export; use `restore-generated-sources.py` to reconstruct authoring PNGs. The source sheets stay independent from the runtime's indexed palette exports.
- Mushmallow and Frostowl add the third follower batch. Pebblit, Bramblebuck, Siltkip and Sunskitter complete the fourth batch; all generated sources are archived byte-for-byte in bounded chunks. Bramblebuck and Siltkip row segmentation uses a higher alpha threshold only for detecting row boundaries because isolated fringe pixels bridge adjacent rows; exported alpha cleanup still follows the shared profile.
- Each runtime atlas is 5 columns × 8 rows: idle, walk-1…walk-4 across all eight directions. Fernling and Duskwing use the standard N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW row order. Emberkin and Brooklet record their generated pose order explicitly in `creature-follower-metadata.json` and the manifest; Brooklet’s two mirrored side rows map its generated left-facing profiles to both world directions. The exporter trims low-alpha noise, fits each complete cell silhouette to a 184 × 184 content box with nearest-neighbor scaling, and aligns the visible feet at y=196 in fixed 200 × 200 cells. Runtime PNGs are 1000 × 1600 and remain separate from the combat atlases.
- The browser preview at `dist/creature-follower-preview.html` displays all seventeen species' frames beside the red-cap player at gameplay scale and runs diagonal-turn, stop, backtrack and depth/foreground scenarios. Follower facing and frame cadence use the follower's own sampled path displacement; each walk frame advances at 0.56 world units, matching the player's stride distance. Walking keeps the authored feet planted without an extra subpixel bob; stopping and reduced motion hold the last direction with idle frame 0. The #131 direction audit corrected the Emberkin west/northwest swap; Brooklet east/southeast swap; Voltkit's south/southeast and west/southwest/northwest rows; Hushram's west/northwest/southeast/southwest rows; and Mushmallow south/southeast rows. These mappings are recorded in both the exporter and runtime manifest.
- `python3 art/characters/export-creature-followers.py --check` verifies all seventeen normalized runtime sheets; `art/characters/creature-follower-metadata.json` records frame order, size, anchor, cadence, prompt summaries and reference species. The static portraits remain fallback if an optional sheet cannot load. The 37 px contact sheet keeps technical visual review separate from pending owner taste and gameplay acceptance.

## First three-creature expansion batch (#286–#288)

Sedgegnaw, Petalunge and Cindercurl use separate stable identity IDs and are not tied to names, dialogue or quest roles. Their original RGBA portrait sources are `creature-sedgegnaw-portrait-generated.png`, `creature-petalunge-portrait-generated.png` and `creature-cindercurl-portrait-generated.png`. The portrait exporter removes alpha values at or below 8, fits each silhouette with nearest-neighbor scaling into a 264 × 264 content box, aligns the feet at y=284 inside a 288 × 288 normalization cell, then trims the runtime image to the visible silhouette. `art/assets/source/creature-additions-atlas.png` retains those tightly cropped outputs with their feet sharing the same bottom line.

The source sheets `creature-sedgegnaw-combat-generated.png`, `creature-petalunge-combat-generated.png` and `creature-cindercurl-combat-generated.png` contain 4 × 5 transparent action grids. Rows are idle, attack, hit, faint and capture; columns are four sequential poses. Each runtime cell is 288 × 288 with a bottom-center foot anchor. Playback uses the shared combat timing in `creature-combat-metadata.json`: idle 220 ms per frame, attack 105 ms, hit 80 ms, faint 160 ms and capture 130 ms. `art/assets/source/creature-combat-new-atlas.png` retains the exact normalized runtime cells for pixel-preserving export.

The source sheets `creature-sedgegnaw-follower-generated.png`, `creature-petalunge-follower-generated.png` and `creature-cindercurl-follower-generated.png` contain five columns × eight rows. Row order is N/NE/E/SE/S/SW/W/NW; columns are idle/walk-1/walk-2/walk-3/walk-4. The normalizer writes 200 × 200 cells, fits each creature inside 184 × 184 with nearest-neighbor sampling and places feet at y=196. Four walk columns advance each 0.56 world units travelled; stop and reduced motion use idle while retaining facing. `art/assets/source/creature-follower-new-atlas.png` retains the exact normalized runtime cells for pixel-preserving export.

The design briefs preserve the six-legged ant and narrow leaf-sail for Sedgegnaw, the narrow triangular head and paired coral-tipped petal forelimbs for Petalunge, and the overlapping kiln plates with shield-tail for Cindercurl. All source files, generation summaries, prompt references, profile hashes and export digests are linked in `art/assets/subjects.json` and the two animation metadata files. The image tool did not provide provider, model-version or seed metadata. `dist/creature-combat-preview.html`, `dist/creature-follower-preview.html`, and the 115 px / 37 px review contact sheets show the new batch at actual game scale. Technical review is recorded separately; owner taste and gameplay acceptance remain pending.
